At the Madrid table

Traditional Madrid dishes and Spanish favourites to try along the way. Every photograph shows the dish described.

Cocido madrileño

A Madrid classic

A substantial chickpea, vegetable and meat stew, traditionally served in courses. A warming lunch for a November day.

Photo: Cocido madrileño de La Clave · Madrid tourism

Bocadillo de calamares

A Madrid classic

A crusty bread roll filled with battered, fried squid rings. A classic snack around Plaza Mayor.

Photo: Bocadillo de calamares. Jesualdo Bermejo, del restaurante Tablafina, en el Hotel NH Madrid Nacional · Madrid tourism

Churros con chocolate

Breakfast or a sweet stop

Fried dough dipped in thick hot chocolate. A classic Madrid breakfast or afternoon treat.

Photo: El desayuno más castizo: churros con chocolate de la Chocolatería San Ginés (©Álvaro López del Cerro). · Madrid tourism

Tortilla de patatas

Spanish classic

A thick potato omelette, with or without onion. Order a pincho for a slice.

Photo: ¿Quieres hacer en casa una tortilla como la de Colósimo? · Madrid tourism

Croquetas

Spanish classic

Crisp, breadcrumbed bites with a creamy filling. Ham is a classic choice; fillings vary.

Photo: Croquetas de Miguel Carretero, Santerra. Foto: Álvaro López del Cerro. © Madrid Destino · Madrid tourism

Patatas bravas

Tapas favourite

Fried potatoes served with a spicy brava sauce. A simple plate to share over drinks.

Photo: Patatas bravas de La Tajada · Madrid tourism

Callos a la madrileña

For adventurous appetites

Madrid-style beef tripe stew, often cooked with chorizo and morcilla. A hearty traditional dish.

Photo: Cazuela de callos, según la receta de Hevia, que se puede degustar en H Emblemático · Madrid tourism

Drinks to try

Caña · Small draught beer

A classic way to order beer in Madrid: a small glass of draught beer, traditionally around 200 ml. Caña is the serving format, rather than a brand; the beer depends on the bar.

For a Madrid beer brand, look for Mahou on tap.

“Una caña, por favor.”

One small draught beer, please.

About Madrid’s tapas & cañas ↗

Tinto de verano

A refreshing Spanish drink made with red wine and lemon soda or lightly sweetened sparkling soda, served over ice and often garnished with a slice of lemon.

“Un tinto de verano, por favor.”

One tinto de verano, please.
